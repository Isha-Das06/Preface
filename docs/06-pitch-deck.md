# Pitch Deck

15 slides. One idea per slide, stated in a sentence you could say out loud.

> **On market data:** I have not researched live market figures, so this deck contains **no TAM number and no adoption statistic.** The spec explicitly said not to fabricate them, and I haven't. Every bracketed `[SOURCE NEEDED]` marks a claim you must verify before showing this to anyone. An unsourced TAM slide is the fastest way to lose a sophisticated investor; a deck with no TAM slide and a sharp wedge is far stronger than one with an invented $47B.

**SLIDE 2 — The problem**
> ## The deal closes. Then nothing happens for nine days.
>
> A service business signs a client. Before work can start they need company details, a questionnaire, brand files, a signed contract, a deposit, and a kickoff call.
>
> Today that's six tools and a spreadsheet to track who did what.
>
> The tools all work. **They just don't know about each other.**

---

**SLIDE 3 — What it actually looks like**
> *A real message thread — Monday through the following Monday, ending in "Sorry to chase again."*
>
> Nine days. Nothing started. This happens on every single client.

*Show, don't summarize. This slide does more work than any other.*

---

**SLIDE 4 — The solution**
> ## One link.
>
> **The client sees** a short checklist. Information → files → contract → deposit → kickoff. No account, no password, works on their phone, saves as they go.
>
> **The business sees** who's finished, who's stalled, and on what. Reminders send themselves.
>
> **When it's done**, one email arrives with everything: answers, files, signed contract, receipt, kickoff time.

>
> Nobody built the layer that runs them in order.
>
> The opportunity isn't another all-in-one. It's a thin workflow layer over tools people already pay for and won't give up.

**SLIDE 9 — Differentiation**
> ## Everything you need to onboard a client. Nothing else.
>
> - **Under 10 minutes** to a live link — competitors take hours to days
> - **No client account** — structurally impossible for portal-based competitors to copy without breaking their own model
> - **"What am I waiting on"** as the home screen, not a report
> - **Nothing moves** — their Stripe, their Calendly, their email
> - **Mobile-first** for the client, because clients fill these in from a phone at 9pm
>
> The pitch to a customer is one line: *you don't have to learn anything.*

---

**SLIDE 10 — Business model**
> Subscription, priced per active onboarding volume.
>
> **$49 · $99 · $199** per month — *initial hypotheses, not validated*
>
> **No transaction fees.** Client payments go directly to the customer's own Stripe account. We never touch the money — which is exactly why they trust us in the contract-and-deposit path.
>
> Expansion: seats, multiple workflows, custom sending domain, white-label.
>
> Rough shape: $99 average → 100 customers ≈ $10k MRR. Gross margin is high; the costs are storage, email, and hosting.

---

**SLIDE 11 — Go to market**
> **The message is a question, not a claim:**
> ### "How many links do you send a new client?"
>
> Everyone answers four or five. That's the whole pitch.
>
> **Channels** — direct outreach to agencies (founder-led, 20/day); agency and consultant communities; a 60-second demo video that shows the client experience; content built around the incident, not the annoyance.
>
> **The trigger to sell against** is a specific failure: work started before the contract came back. A deposit never collected. A client who went quiet for three weeks. Nobody switches tools over mild inconvenience.

---

**SLIDE 12 — Roadmap**
> **Now** — templates, forms, files, signing, payments, scheduling, reminders, dashboard, handoff
> **Next** — multiple workflows, custom sending domain, Slack and Drive delivery, saved templates
> **Later** — API and webhooks, white-label, analytics, conditional steps
>
> AI generates a starting workflow from a description of your business. **A convenience at setup, not the product.** We are not an AI company and won't market as one.

---

**SLIDE 13 — Vision**
> ## Become the default step between "client signed" and "work started."
>
> Not a business operating system. One workflow, done properly, for every service business that has one.
>
> Every service business should be able to send one link and know exactly what's left before work begins.

**SLIDE 14 — Why this can win**
> The category is already proven — businesses pay for HoneyBook, Dubsado and Bonsai today. We don't have to convince anyone that onboarding matters.
>
> Every incumbent is growing broader. That's rational for them and it opens a gap underneath: the customer who wants this *one thing* solved and refuses to migrate their business to get it.
>
> **The wedge is focus, speed, and the discipline to keep saying no.**
>
> **Honest risks** — the biggest is not competition, it's whether the pain is acute enough to trigger a switch. We're testing that with paid customers before building anything beyond the MVP. Second: onboarding could be a feature rather than a company. Our answer is that the customer who rejects all-in-one is a real and durable segment — but a smaller one, and we're choosing it deliberately.

## Delivery notes

- **Slides 3 and 5 carry the deck.** The thread and the demo do the persuading; everything else is scaffolding. If you only have five minutes, show those two.
- **Slide 14's honesty is a feature.** Naming your weakest assumption before the investor does converts skepticism into credibility. Do not remove it to look stronger.
- **Do not add a TAM slide** until you have a defensible bottom-up number: (service businesses in band) × (plausible penetration) × (ARPU). Top-down market sizing from an analyst PDF reads as unserious.
- **Traction beats everything here.** Ten paying customers makes slides 6–8 nearly unnecessary. Prioritize getting them over polishing this.
